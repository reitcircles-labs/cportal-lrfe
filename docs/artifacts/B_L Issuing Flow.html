B/L Issuing Flow
Design for review · epic CB-8 · ticket CB-11

Issuing a bill of lading or a sea waybill

Steps 1 to 3 of the CB-9 map, screen by screen: the shipper's instructions, the draft and its corrections, the carrier's four-eyes approval, and the issue that creates the one electronic original and names its first holder. The same flow makes a sea waybill, a straight B/L and a B/L to order.

What this flow assumes

CB-9's five decisions are not agreed yet. This page takes its recommendations as given; if you change one, the steps marked below change with it.

  • A transfer needs the receiver's acceptance (CB-9 question 1). Issue is not a transfer: the first holder does not have to accept, since the shipper asked for the B/L (see question 4 below).
  • Reference law: UK Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023, as a country setting (question 2).
  • A forwarder does not hold title in v1 (question 3): Tomás prepares and reviews, Ana's company is the first holder.
  • A neutral operator runs the platform (question 4), and the eleven personas stand (question 5).
Part 1

Goal and people

Goal: turn the shipper's instructions into one issued, signed electronic transport document that matches what the shipper asked for, is issued once only for that cargo, and whose issue anyone can check on the blockchain. For a B/L, the issue also names the first holder.

Part 4

What is anchored, and how a duplicate is stopped

On the blockchain at issue

  • The B/L or waybill number and its type.
  • The fingerprint (hash) of the sealed document, so any copy can be compared with the original.
  • The issuing carrier and the first holder, as platform organisation ids, not names.
  • A cargo key: a salted fingerprint of carrier, container numbers and seal numbers (question 5).
  • The time and the reference law (country setting).

Never on the blockchain: the instructions, drafts, comments, goods, weights, prices, or the letter of credit. Those stay in the platform and its audit trail.

One issue per cargo

  • When the draft is made and again at approval, the platform looks for any draft or issued document of the same carrier with the same container and seal numbers.
  • An issued match blocks the issue. A draft match warns Lena and links the other draft.
  • The same booking reference alone only warns: one booking can lawfully split into several B/Ls.
  • The cargo key on the blockchain lets a bank or court see that no second issue used the same key, without seeing the cargo.
  • Replacing an issued document (amendment, switch to paper) is CB-14; the old one is marked replaced, never issued twice.

One electronic original

  • The document says "Number of originals: one (1)". There is no "full set of three" in electronic form.
  • Every PDF or printout is marked "Not an original" and shows the check code.
  • A credit asking for "full set 3/3" is met by the one electronic record under eUCP to confirm article and bank practice
Part 5

Errors, refusals and disputes

Red: the flow stops until someone acts. Amber: a warning the user can deal with and go on.

The automatic reading is unsure or contradicts itself (1.2b)

Fields under the confidence threshold, or two values on different pages, are marked for Ana. Sending stays off until she confirms each one.

"3 fields need your check. Gross weight: page 1 says 38,400 kg, page 2 says 38,040 kg."

Instructions incomplete

Missing consignee, ports, containers or goods description: the form names each one. For a straight B/L or waybill, the consignee must be a named party; "to order" is refused.

"A straight B/L needs a named consignee. Choose 'B/L to order' if the consignee is 'to order'."

Forwarder acts beyond delegation

Tomás can prepare but not submit or approve when Ana's company did not delegate it (CB-10). The button is replaced by a request to Ana.

"Café Serra Alta approves its own drafts. Ana Ribeiro has been asked to approve version 2."

Shipper asks for corrections the carrier refuses

Lena answers the comment instead of changing the field (for example, a clause the carrier must keep, or a weight the carrier cannot confirm). Ana may approve anyway or keep asking. Discussion stays on the field, both sides see it.

On Ana's draft: "Lena: we keep 'shipper's load, stow and count' on all FCL shipments."

The shipper does not answer

Lena sees how long the draft has waited and can send a reminder. Nothing is issued without the shipper's approval of the current version.

"With shipper for 2 days · vessel sails 10 Nov · Send reminder"

The draft changes after the shipper approved it

Any change other than adding the on-board date clears the approval and sends the draft back to Ana.

To Ravi: the "Text identical to the approved version" check fails and issuing is off.

Ravi sends it back

Ravi gives a reason; the draft returns to Lena, keeps the shipper's approval only if Lena changes nothing.

Lena: "Sent back by Ravi Menon: on-board date is before the vessel's departure from the previous port."

Same person tries to prepare and approve

Whoever edited the draft cannot approve it, even with the approver role (the platform's existing four-eyes rule).

"You prepared this draft. Another approver at Nordsee Line must issue it."

Duplicate cargo

Containers and seals already on an issued document of this carrier: issue is blocked, with a link to the other document for carrier staff only.

"MSKU 704512-3 with seal SL 220871 is already on CBL-2026-000098 (issued 2 Nov). Issue is blocked. To replace a document, use an amendment."

First holder not on the platform

An electronic B/L needs a holder who can hold it. The carrier can invite the shipper's organisation (CB-10) or issue on paper outside the platform; no holder-less B/L is issued. A waybill has no holder and is not affected.

"Café Serra Alta is not on the platform yet. Invite them, or issue this B/L on paper."

The carrier's system sends a bad or duplicate issue request (1.2c)

A DCSA issuance request with missing data, an unknown holder, or a duplicate cargo key is refused with a coded answer to that system, and listed for Lena.

Lena's list: "Refused from system: duplicate of CBL-2026-000098".

The blockchain proof is slow

The document is issued and signed at 3.3; the proof follows. Until it is confirmed, the holder sees "proof pending" and cannot offer a transfer, so no title step runs ahead of its proof.

"Issued. Proof on the blockchain: pending (usually a few minutes). You can endorse once it is confirmed."

A mistake found after issue

An issued document is never edited or deleted. Corrections go through an amendment agreed by holder and carrier (CB-14).

On the issued page: "Ask for an amendment".

  • Four-eyes: one person prepares, another issues. Each carrier's organisation admin sets who holds the clerk and approver roles at the carrier (CB-10).
  • Private until issue: instructions, drafts and comments are seen only by the shipper's side and the carrier. The audit trail keeps every version, comment and approval with its author and time.

Part 7

Decisions for you

A recommendation is given for each; tell me where you disagree.

1. May the forwarder approve the draft for the shipper?

Options: (a) only the shipper approves; (b) the forwarder may, when the shipper delegated draft approval.

Recommended: (b). It is how forwarders work today; the shipper sees who approved, and the delegation is set by the shipper's own admin in CB-10. The first holder is still the shipper.

2. Shipped on board only, or also "received for shipment"?

Options: (a) issue only after loading, with the on-board date; (b) also allow "received for shipment" B/Ls with a later on-board notation.

Recommended: (a) in v1. Letters of credit almost always ask for an on-board B/L; (b) adds a notation step and can come with amendments (CB-14).

3. When the carrier's own system issues (DCSA issuance), who does the four-eyes?

Options: (a) the request arrives as an approved draft and Ravi still issues on the platform; (b) the carrier's system issues straight through, its own controls counting as the four-eyes.

Recommended: (a) for the first version. The platform can prove its own four-eyes; it cannot prove the carrier system's. Straight-through can be a later carrier setting.

4. Does the first holder accept the issued B/L?

Options: (a) the shipper becomes holder at issue; (b) the shipper must accept, like a transfer.

Recommended: (a). The shipper asked for the B/L and approved its text; one more click adds delay without protecting anyone.

5. What counts as "the same cargo", and how much goes on the blockchain?

Options: (a) carrier + container + seal numbers, anchored as a salted fingerprint; (b) the booking reference only; (c) no cargo key on chain, duplicates checked only inside the platform.

Recommended: (a). Container and seal identify the physical cargo; a booking can be split. The salted fingerprint lets a bank or court see that no second issue exists, without exposing the cargo. to confirm: LCL and break-bulk cargo need another key
